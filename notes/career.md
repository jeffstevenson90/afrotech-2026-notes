## Summary in plain terms

A practical career talk for students, early-career people and those affected by layoffs. The formula: **build skills → show proof → build relationships → be visible → opportunities follow,** paired with a 12-month plan and a push to find sponsors, not just mentors.

## Key callouts

- **The 12-month plan:** months 1–3 learn one new skill; 4–6 build a project with it; 7–9 build your network in that space; 10–12 apply and interview.
- **Not every opportunity pays at first.** He built his reputation working events and tours for free early on, which led to paid work with major artists and labels.
- **You don't need it all figured out, but you need a strategy,** including for "what if I'm not employed."
- **Mentors vs. sponsors.** Mentors advise you; sponsors advocate for you in rooms you're not in. A VP sponsor got him promoted 7 months into a role after he took on the target job as a "plus one" alongside his day job.
- **Cold outreach works when it's confident and followed up.**
- **Give, don't just take.** Ask senior people "What can I help you with?"
- **Future-proof by moving with change.** Pitch new ideas with data showing how they cut workload *and* make money.
- **Moving internally:** build relationships in the team you want and be visibly curious.

## Learnings and signals for the team

- **Each team member can build a 12-month plan:** one skill, one project, a network, then a stretch opportunity.
- **Be sponsors for the team** in calibration and talent conversations.
- **Offer "plus one" stretch assignments** deliberately and recognize them.
- **Pitch with data and dollars.**
