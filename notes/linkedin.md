## Summary in plain terms

Two people who build LinkedIn's feed explained how it works and how to get discovered on it. Both took nonlinear paths into tech. Their message: post your work and insights consistently, and engage in conversations instead of just scrolling. The feed matches people sharing knowledge with people looking for it.

## Key callouts

- **"Switch hitters" win in the AI era.** People from other fields bring an outside perspective, then use AI tools to cover product, design and engineering work.
- **Roles are going end to end.** Travis now defines the idea, labels data, uses language models to detect and evaluate comment quality, and submits the code change himself.
- **The cost of building is falling fast.** Learn the tools, build, and share publicly.
- **The feed is a "knowledge marketplace"** that works best when people both consume and create, with some exploration outside your field.
- **Commenting pays off.** People who comment are 4–7x more likely to report a great experience, and engagement leads to opportunities.
- **Scale:** about 700 million comment impressions a day, ranked through a "funnel" built on embeddings.
- **Leave "breadcrumbs" for others** by sharing pieces of your career path.

## Learnings and signals for the team

- **Expect end-to-end ownership;** encourage consultants to build and test, not just spec and hand off.
- **Value non-traditional backgrounds** when hiring and staffing.
- **Build the team's visibility** by sharing work and insights within company policy.
- **Familiar ML patterns** (embeddings, ranking funnels, LLM quality classifiers) are a useful reference for recommendation or classification work.
