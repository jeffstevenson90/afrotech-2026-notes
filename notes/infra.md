## Summary in plain terms

Two senior tech leaders on how AI is quietly rebuilding the systems behind companies: code writing code, AI testing software, and systems predicting the "next best action." AI is just prediction from patterns. Leaders need to pick the right kind of AI for each job, test it for bias, get the whole workforce using it, and take responsibility for reskilling people as roles change.

## Key callouts

- **Demystify AI: it's prediction.** It anticipates the next action from patterns, like autocomplete.
- **Deterministic vs. probabilistic AI.** Some use cases must give the same right answer every time ("1 + 1 = 2"); those need deterministic approaches.
- **AI checking AI.** Workday runs deterministic checks for accuracy, bias, toxicity and tone on its AI.
- **Automate the routine, elevate the people.** AI answers well-documented questions; people spot patterns like related incidents pointing to a bigger outage.
- **Get everyone using AI, with no pressure on output.** Workday gave every employee an AI assistant in early 2023 and told them to experiment freely. Strong use cases came from the ground up.
- **Bias comes from who builds and tests.** Face filters and automatic sinks that failed on darker skin; AI that handled cultural context poorly until given it.
- **Creativity is your differentiator.** AI reproduces known patterns; standout work comes from you.

## The leadership experiment: one person, four roles

One panelist (about 800 employees and 1,000 contractors) said the roles changing most are **product manager, business analyst, solution architect and QA**. She had one person use AI to cover all four on a project: delivery was **3–5x faster with better quality**, every time.

The gain came from **ownership**: one person with total responsibility and no handoffs across roles and regions. People attached to their title struggled; people who stretched thrived and were paid more.

## Leaders' responsibility

- **Signal change early and invest in reskilling.** She told contractor agencies to reskill QA workers for what's next.
- **Redeploy before you cut.** A rotation program, CEO-backed roles and early HR involvement for a team whose work was going away; later rolled out company-wide.
- **Don't wait for AI to happen to you.** Be creators, not just users.

## Learnings and signals for the team

- **Expect BI roles to converge.** Encourage stretching across requirements, design, build and testing, and owning outcomes end to end.
- **Use deterministic logic where answers must be exact,** and label generative output clearly.
- **Build AI quality checks** (accuracy, bias, tone) on AI-generated outputs.
- **Test for who's missing** before release.
- **Encourage open experimentation** without output targets.
