## Summary in plain terms

A 10-year anniversary welcome. The host urged attendees to build real relationships with the people sitting next to them. Morgan DeBaun told AfroTech's origin story and made the case that the AI era is still being written, so the community should actively shape it rather than wait for a turn.

## Key callouts

- **From 650 to 5,000+.** Blavity launched in 2014 from her parents' house. The first AfroTech (2016, San Francisco) drew about 650 people; this year's main room alone seats over 5,000, across seven stages.
- **The future isn't finished.** Amazon, Google and ChatGPT all launched within our lifetimes. The rules on AI, data and who's protected are still being written.
- **"Not what will technology do to us, but what will we do with technology."** Start on the problems you've complained about for years. Be human-centered.
- **The Double Dutch metaphor.** The ropes don't slow down so you can step in comfortably. Practice the rhythm, accept you might catch a rope on your ankles, and jump in. And it takes at least three people: you can't play alone.
- **Networking is a long game.** Authentic connections become the people who trust you and pick up the phone years later.
- **Don't wait your turn.** In every major shift, the rules for who builds, works and owns get rewritten.

## Learnings and signals for the team

- **Jump in on AI now, imperfectly.** Start small and accept early stumbles.
- **Bring the problems we already complain about.** Recurring pain points are the best starting list for AI projects.
- **Build relationships across the business** before we need them.
