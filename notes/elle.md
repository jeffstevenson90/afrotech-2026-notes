## Summary in plain terms

J. Elle talked about her career in publishing as a Black author: the post-2020 surge of interest in Black stories and the quiet pullback that followed. She covered how she learned to advocate for herself, and the ideas behind her new "romantasy" built around a high-stakes dating competition, which uses "engineered happiness" as a stand-in for the algorithms and attention economy that shape what we see online.

## Key callouts

- **The post-2020 surge faded.** Black creators got a wave of attention; within a few years it quieted and the market shifted.
- **Metrics can miss value.** Decisions about which books get marketing money rely on metrics that don't capture the audience. The ask is "a chair as big as yours" at the main table.
- **Pick representation you trust.** Your champion has more leverage than you alone. The wrong representation is worse than none.
- **Don't act "just lucky to be here."** She left a first agent she was grateful to because she could see where she wanted to go.
- **The attention economy is a design choice.** Corporations decide what's put in front of us; think critically about it.
- **Build a career, not one hit.** Reinvent with each project; a first draft is just a first draft.

## Learnings and signals for the team

- **Question what our metrics leave out.** Dashboards can steer investment away from audiences the data under-represents.
- **Algorithmic curation draws scrutiny.** Be transparent about how any ranking or recommendation works.
- **Encourage the team to advocate for their work** and find sponsors they trust.
