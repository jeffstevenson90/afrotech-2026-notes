**Speakers:** Dr. Timnit Gebru, founder of the Distributed AI Research Institute (DAIR), with a former Google colleague who led Google's skin-tone research (name unclear) · **Note:** figures are as stated on stage.

## Summary in plain terms

A frank conversation about who builds AI, who it serves and who pays the cost. Dr. Gebru argued that "AI" is mostly a marketing label, that every AI system rests on human labor and human-chosen data, and that companies must be held accountable for what their systems do. Superintelligence hype pulls attention away from real, present-day harms and regulation.

## Key callouts

- **"AI" is a marketing term** (coined in 1956) that runs through hype cycles. Be specific about what technology you mean.
- **It's people all the way down.** Hundreds of millions of data workers label and clean data and moderate content, often for around $1/hour.
- **Data is the most important and least glamorous part.** Her 2018 "Gender Shades" research with Dr. Joy Buolamwini found facial recognition worked worst for darker-skinned women. Later, a Black man in Detroit was wrongfully arrested because of it.
- **"What's in the soup?"** A restaurant that can't name its ingredients wouldn't be trusted. Companies scraping the internet often can't document their training data either.
- **Nothing fundamental changed with LLMs;** the same data problems now play out at larger scale.
- **Accountability, not "the model went rogue."** The moderator cited a California law barring "the AI did it" as a defense.
- **Prove safety before release,** as every other industry must.
- **Don't accept hype as fact.** Layoffs justified by AI often reflect a gap between what AI can do and what leaders believe.
- **Tech isn't neutral.** Demand transparency about AI use and training data.

## Learnings and signals for the team

- **Know your ingredients.** Document data sources, lineage and known gaps for every dataset and model.
- **Check accuracy by group, not just overall.**
- **Own the outcome.** Keep a named human owner for every AI-assisted output.
- **Be specific and skeptical with vendors** about what their AI does and what data trained it.
- **Be transparent about AI use** in reports and tools.
